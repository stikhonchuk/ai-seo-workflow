## SEO Writing Best Practices

### 1. Title Tag Optimization
**Format:** Primary Keyword | Secondary Context | [YOUR-BRAND]
**Length:** 50-60 characters
**Examples:**
- Best SEO Tools 2026 | Free Tools Compared | [YOUR-BRAND]
- How to Choose a Digital Agency: Complete Guide | [YOUR-BRAND] Blog
- PPC Campaign Examples for E-commerce | [YOUR-BRAND]

### 2. Meta Description Optimization
**Length:** 150-160 characters
**Include:** Primary keyword, benefit, call-to-action
**Examples:**
- "Learn how to run effective SEO campaigns with our step-by-step guide. Free checklists and templates included. Get a free consultation from [YOUR-BRAND]."
- "Compare digital marketing strategies for 2026. See how The client's data-driven approach delivers 2x profit growth. Get free consultation."

### 3. URL Structure
**Best Practices:**
- Keep URLs short and descriptive
- Use hyphens to separate words
- Include primary keyword
- Use lowercase only

**Good Examples:**
- /blog/seo-audit-checklist
- /blog/how-to-choose-digital-agency
- /blog/[your-brand]-vs-[competitor]
- /blog/ecommerce-marketing-guide

**Bad Examples:**
- /blog/post-12345
- /blog/best-seo-tools-2026

### 4. Heading Structure (H1-H6)

**Rules:**
- Only ONE H1 per page (page title)
- Use H2 for main sections
- Use H3 for subsections under H2
- Include keywords naturally in headings
- Make headings descriptive and clear

**Example Structure:**
```
H1: PPC Campaign Examples for E-commerce in 2026
  H2: What Makes a Good SEO Strategy?
    H3: Components of an Effective SEO Campaign
    H3: Common SEO Mistakes to Avoid
  H2: 10 SEO Strategy Examples
    H3: 1. Local SEO Strategy
    H3: 2. E-commerce SEO Strategy
  H2: How to Track SEO Performance
    H3: Using [YOUR-BRAND] for Marketing Goals
  H2: Frequently Asked Questions
```

### 5. Keyword Usage

**Keyword Density:** 0.7-1.0% for primary keyword (natural distribution, never forced)
- **Target 0.8% on first draft** — that's ~20 occurrences per 2,500 words.
- Never force the keyword into a sentence where "the app", the brand name, or a pronoun reads better. Readability always wins over density targets.
- If a sentence only exists to carry the keyword, delete it or rewrite it with actual value.
- Secondary keywords: 0.2-0.5% each (5-12 occurrences per 2,500 words).
- Distribute keywords across ALL article zones: intro, each H2 section, FAQ, conclusion.
- **Write frontmatter (meta_title, meta_description) AFTER the article body** to ensure promises match content.

**Where to Include Primary Keyword:**
- Title tag (H1)
- First paragraph (within first 100 words)
- At least one H2 heading
- URL
- Meta description
- Alt text (at least one image)
- Conclusion paragraph

**Where to Include Secondary Keywords:**
- H2 and H3 headings
- Throughout body content
- Image alt text
- Internal link anchor text

**Keyword Variations:**
Use natural variations and synonyms:
- Primary: "SEO agency"
- Variations: "SEO company", "digital marketing agency", "SEO services"

### 6. Content Length Guidelines

**Blog Articles:**
- Short guides: 800-1,200 words
- Standard articles: 1,500-2,000 words
- Comprehensive guides: 2,500-3,500 words
- Pillar content: 3,500+ words

**Landing/Feature Pages:**
- Minimum: 500 words of unique content
- Recommended: 800-1,200 words
- Feature pages: 400-600 words of unique content

### 7. Content Localization Rules

**EN articles ([your-domain.com]):**
- Do NOT mention [your-domain.ru], Russian pricing (₽), Russian phone numbers, or the Russian version
- Keep content focused on the international audience
- Use USD pricing only

**RU articles ([your-domain.ru]):**
- Do NOT mention [your-domain.com] or international/USD pricing
- Keep content focused on the Russian audience
- Use RUB (₽) pricing only

Each language version is independent — no cross-references between markets.

### 8. Internal Linking Strategy

**Guidelines:**
- Include 3-5 internal links per article
- Link to related articles and service pages
- Use descriptive anchor text (not "click here")
- Link to pillar content from supporting articles
- **Only link to published pages** — never link to articles that haven't been published yet. For planned future content, add an HTML comment `<!-- TODO: add link to /blog/[slug] when published -->` instead

**Anchor Text Examples:**
- Good: "free SEO audit checklist"
- Good: "our complete guide to SEO optimization"
- Good: "see how teams use goal hierarchy"
- Bad: "click here"
- Bad: "read more"

### 8. External Linking

**Best Practices:**
- Link to 1-2 authoritative sources per article
- Link to research, methodology sources, or industry publications
- Use "nofollow" for competitor links
- Open external links in new tab

**Good External Link Sources:**
- Google (Webmaster Guidelines, Search Quality Rater Guidelines)
- Harvard Business Review (management research)
- HubSpot (Inbound marketing methodology)
- Industry reports on digital marketing effectiveness
- Academic research on team performance

### 9. Image Optimization

#### Hero Image Requirements (MANDATORY)

Every blog article MUST have a unique hero image before publication.

**Specifications:**
- **Aspect ratio:** 16:9 (1920x1080px recommended)
- **Style:** Modern professional agency aesthetic, clean and professional
- **No text on images** — Text overlays added in CMS
- **No people's faces** — Avoid licensing complications
- **No brand logos** — Keep images brand-neutral

**Image Types for the client Content:**
- **Dashboard mockups:** Generic analytics/marketing dashboards (not actual client branding)
- **Abstract goal visualization:** Progress bars, checkmarks, milestone markers, ascending steps
- **Team collaboration:** People around devices viewing dashboards (faces out of frame)
- **Diagrams:** Marketing funnel, campaign structure, strategy frameworks
- **Modern workspace:** Clean desk with laptop showing goal interface

**AI Image Generation:**
- **Recommended tools:** Midjourney, DALL-E 3, Stable Diffusion, Leonardo.ai
- **Consistency:** Use same AI tool for all images in a content batch

**Hero Image Prompt Template:**
```
[Scene description], [goal/productivity context], [setting], [lighting], [photography/illustration style], [color palette], [mood]. No people's faces, no text, no logos. 16:9 aspect ratio.
```

**Example Prompt:**
```
Modern dashboard interface showing marketing campaign performance metrics and conversion funnels, clean white background with subtle blue accents, professional analytics UI design, soft ambient lighting, flat design illustration style, blue and green color palette, productive and focused atmosphere. No text, no logos, no faces. 16:9 aspect ratio.
```

**Recommended Color Palette for the client:**
- Primary: Professional blues (#2563EB, #3B82F6)
- Success: Goal green (#10B981, #059669)
- Accent: Warm amber (#F59E0B)
- Neutral: Clean grays (#6B7280, #F3F4F6)
- Background: White/light gray (#FFFFFF, #F9FAFB)

#### General Image Optimization

**File Names:**
- Use descriptive names with keywords
- Good: marketing-funnel-diagram.png
- Bad: IMG_12345.jpg

**Alt Text:**
- Describe the image clearly
- Include relevant keywords naturally
- Good: "Marketing funnel diagram showing awareness, consideration, and conversion stages"
- Bad: "image"

**File Size:**
- Compress images to under 100KB when possible
- Use WebP format for better compression
- Lazy load images below the fold

### 10. Call-to-Action (CTA) Best Practices

**Placement:**
- Within first 2 paragraphs (soft CTA)
- Middle of content (secondary CTA)
- End of content (primary CTA)

**CTA Examples (English):**
- → See client.md#ctas for specific CTA text
- "Get Your Free Audit"
- "Start Setting Goals Today"
- "Get Started — It's Free"
- "See How It Works"

**CTA Examples (Russian):**
- "Попробуйте [YOUR-BRAND] бесплатно"
- → See client.md#ctas
- "Начните ставить цели"
- "Начните бесплатно"
- "Посмотрите, как это работает"

**CTA Placement Rules:**
1. **After value delivery**: Place CTAs after providing useful information
2. **Decision points**: After comparisons, recommendations
3. **Natural breaks**: End of major sections
4. **Not too early**: First CTA after 200+ words minimum
5. **Not too frequent**: Maximum 1 CTA per 500 words

## Writing Style Guide

### Tone & Voice
- **Professional but approachable:** Expert knowledge without jargon
- **Helpful and educational:** Focus on solving problems
- **Confident but not pushy:** Make recommendations without hard selling
- **Clear and concise:** Get to the point quickly

### Writing Tips

**Do:**
- Write in second person ("you") to connect with readers
- Use short paragraphs (3-4 sentences max)
- Include bullet points and lists for scannability
- Break up text with subheadings every 300 words
- Use active voice ("Set your goals" vs "Goals should be set")
- Include specific examples and templates
- Back up claims with data or methodology sources

**Don't:**
- Use jargon or technical terms without explanation
- Write long, rambling paragraphs
- Stuff keywords unnaturally
- Make claims you can't support
- Copy content from other websites
- Use clickbait or misleading titles
- Include irrelevant information

### Bilingual Content Considerations

**English Content:**
- Primary target: Google
- Tone: Direct, action-oriented
- Marketing terminology: Use standard English terms

**Russian Content:**
- Primary target: Google + Yandex
- Tone: Professional but slightly more formal than English
- Marketing terms: "SEO", "PPC", "CPA" used as-is (Latin), Russian equivalents for explanation
- Use "вы" (formal) throughout
- Consider cultural context: digital marketing practices vary between EN and RU markets
- Include metric measurements
- Yandex values content freshness highly

## Content Creation Workflow

Follow this workflow for every piece of content:

### Phase 1: Planning
1. Choose content piece from publication calendar
2. Review the content brief (keywords, intent, word count)
3. Select appropriate template from this guide
4. Create AI image prompts (hero + supporting images)

### Phase 2: Writing
5. Write first draft focusing on value and clarity
6. Include all required elements (CTAs, links, schema)
7. Save draft to `content/drafts/` directory

### Phase 3: Review (MANDATORY)
8. **Run `/review-article`** to get multi-critic evaluation
   - This runs 7 specialized critics automatically
   - Generates detailed review with scores and fixes

9. Review the critic report:
   - **Score ≥8**: Ready for final review
   - **Score 6-7**: Address major issues, re-run review
   - **Score <6**: Significant revision needed

10. Fix issues identified by critics (priority order):
    - Critical issues first
    - Major issues second
    - Minor issues if time allows

11. Re-run `/review-article` after fixes to verify improvement

### Phase 4: Publication
12. Generate images using AI prompts
13. Final human review (user checks language quality)
14. Upload to CMS with all metadata
15. Submit to Google Search Console and Yandex Webmaster

### Phase 5: Monitoring
16. Track initial rankings (Week 1)
17. Monitor Search Console/Yandex Webmaster (Week 2-4)
18. Update content if needed based on performance

## Critic Review System

### Available Critics

Run `/review-article` to automatically evaluate content across 7 dimensions:

| Critic | Model | What It Checks |
|--------|-------|----------------|
| **SEO** | Sonnet | Keywords, meta tags, structure, links, schema |
| **Russian Language** | Opus | Grammar, natural phrasing, register, calques |
| **English Language** | Opus | Grammar, clarity, technical accuracy |
| **E-E-A-T** | Sonnet | Experience, expertise, authority, trust signals |
| **User Intent** | Sonnet | Search intent satisfaction, completeness |
| **Readability** | Haiku | Sentence/paragraph structure, scannability |
| **Commercial** | Haiku | CTAs, feature links, conversion path |
| **Image Prompts** | Haiku | AI prompt validation, brand consistency |

### Review Commands

```bash
# Full review (all critics)
/review-article

# Quick review (summary scores only - faster/cheaper)
/review-article --quick

# Single critic (for targeted fixes)
/review-article --critic=seo
/review-article --critic=russian
/review-article --critic=english
/review-article --critic=eeat
/review-article --critic=intent
/review-article --critic=readability
/review-article --critic=commercial
/review-article --critic=images
```

### Score Interpretation

| Overall Score | Status | Action |
|---------------|--------|--------|
| 9-10 | Excellent | Ready to publish |
| 8 | Good | Minor polish, then publish |
| 6-7 | Needs Work | Fix issues, re-review |
| 4-5 | Major Issues | Significant revision needed |
| <4 | Rewrite | Start over with new approach |

### Review Output Location

Reviews are saved to: `content/reviews/review-[filename]-[date].md`
